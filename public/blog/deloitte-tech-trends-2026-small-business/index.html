What Deloitte's 2026 Tech Trends Report Means for Small Business

Deloitte just released its 17th annual Tech Trends report, and the headline is not subtle: AI experimentation is over. The organizations that are winning right now are the ones that moved from pilots to production — and built infrastructure that can hold the weight of what they deployed.

That framing is aimed at enterprise leaders. But the five forces Deloitte identifies apply at every scale. The gap between businesses that understood this shift early and those that did not is widening. Here is what the report actually says — and what it means if you are running a business without a strong IT department behind you.

Trend 1: AI Goes Physical — Robots Are Leaving the Factory Floor

Deloitte's first trend is the convergence of AI and robotics. AI-enabled robots are scaling rapidly in manufacturing and logistics, moving from narrow pre-programmed tools to adaptive systems that can sense, decide, act, and learn. The humanoid form factor is next.

For most small businesses, this is a horizon trend — not a decision you make this quarter. But the signal matters. The same AI reasoning that will power a warehouse robot is already available to you as an agent. The underlying capability has arrived. The physical embodiment is following.

What this means for you

Watch the labor-intensive parts of your business. The industries being transformed first — logistics, field service, construction — are adjacent to businesses that depend on them. Your supply chain, your contractors, your vendors: their cost structures are about to change. That is worth tracking.

Trend 2: The Agentic Reality Check — Only 11% Have Actually Deployed

This is the most important number in the entire report. Deloitte found that only 11% of organizations have deployed AI agents in production. Not piloting. Not testing. Actually running them.

The reason is not the technology. The technology works. The barrier is that most organizations are trying to automate processes that were built for people — and AI agents do not perform well inside workflows designed around human limitations. You cannot bolt an agent onto a broken process and call it transformation. You have to redesign the process first.

Deloitte calls this the shift to agent-first architecture. Leaders are building hybrid teams of humans and digital workers — and they are starting to manage those teams with HR-style practices: clear roles, defined escalation paths, oversight protocols. An agent is not a tool you deploy. It is a worker you manage.

What this means for you

If you have explored an AI agent and it underperformed, the process — not the model — is usually why. Before deploying anything, map the workflow from scratch as if no human were in it. What would an agent need to know? Where does it need to escalate? What does a successful outcome look like? Answer those questions first, then build.

Trend 3: The AI Infrastructure Reckoning — Costs Are Rising Faster Than Budgets

AI processing costs have dropped — but usage is growing far faster. Deloitte found that many organizations are now facing significant monthly infrastructure bills they did not anticipate when they started. Cloud services built for traditional workloads are becoming too expensive at AI scale, and systems are being run on aging infrastructure that was never designed for inference-heavy applications.

Leading organizations are responding with three-tier hybrid architectures — cloud, on-premises, and edge computing working together. Some are finding it faster and cheaper to build purpose-built AI data centers from scratch than to retrofit what exists.

What this means for you

You are almost certainly running cloud-based AI tools right now. That is fine — it is the right starting point. But know your costs at the usage level, not just the subscription level. As you scale, the gap between what you are spending and what you are getting will determine whether your AI infrastructure is an asset or a drain. Track it from day one.

Trend 4: The Great Rebuild — IT Is Being Redesigned from the Foundation Up

Deloitte's fourth trend is the most structural. AI is not just changing what IT departments do — it is changing what they are. The organizations gaining advantage are redesigning the technology function itself: leaner, faster, modular. They are tying every AI initiative to a measurable business outcome and rebuilding talent strategies around human-machine collaboration.

According to Deloitte's research, 78% of tech leaders anticipate integrating AI agents directly into their architecture workflows. That is not a future aspiration. That is a near-term operating reality for the organizations that intend to stay competitive.

Incremental improvement is no longer sufficient. The report is direct about this: the pace of AI innovation has created a multiplying flywheel. Generative AI reached 100 million users in two months — a milestone that took the telephone 50 years. Traditional processes built on sequential improvement cannot keep pace with that acceleration.

What this means for you

As a small business, you do not have a legacy IT department to rebuild — which is an advantage, not a limitation. You can build AI-native from the start. That means every new workflow you build should be designed with AI as a participant, not an afterthought. The businesses that treat AI as a feature bolt-on will spend the next two years catching up to the ones that built it in.

Trend 5: The AI Dilemma — AI Is Both the Threat and the Defense

The fifth trend is the one that gets underestimated until something goes wrong. AI creates new security risks: shadow AI deployments, adversarial attacks on AI models, and a significantly wider attack surface than traditional software. At the same time, AI is becoming one of the most powerful tools for cyber defense — through automated threat detection, adversarial training, and AI-driven red teaming.

Deloitte's recommendation is to pair "AI for cyber" with "cyber for AI" — meaning that every AI deployment needs its own security blueprint, not just a reference to the organization's existing security policy.

What this means for you

Every AI tool in your business that has access to client data, internal processes, or communication channels is a potential attack surface. That is not a reason to avoid AI — it is a reason to be deliberate about it. Know what each tool can access, who controls it, and what happens if it is compromised. That discipline does not require a security team. It requires the same attention you give to any vendor with access to your business.

The Actual Takeaway: The Pilot Era Is Closed

Deloitte's report is a clear signal: the businesses that treated 2024 and 2025 as years to explore are now behind the businesses that treated those years as years to build.

That does not mean you cannot close the gap. It means the gap is real, it is documented, and it is growing. The five trends Deloitte identified are not predictions. They are descriptions of what is already happening in the organizations that invested early and built correctly.

The good news for small businesses: you do not need enterprise infrastructure to move from experimentation to impact. You need a specific problem, a clean workflow, an agent designed for that workflow, and a human who stays in the loop for the decisions that matter. That is achievable right now, at any scale.

The window for deliberate, well-built AI infrastructure is open — and the tools will keep getting better. The businesses building now will simply have more to work with as they do.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are Deloitte's five tech trends for 2026?

Deloitte's 2026 Tech Trends report identifies five interconnected forces: AI-enabled robotics moving into physical environments, agentic AI building hybrid human-digital workforces, an AI infrastructure reckoning as costs rise faster than budgets, a full rebuild of IT operating models around AI-native architecture, and a cyber security paradox where AI both creates and defends against new threats. Source: Deloitte Tech Trends 2026 press release.

Does Deloitte's 2026 tech report apply to small businesses?

Yes. The five trends Deloitte identifies apply at every scale. The underlying forces — agentic AI, infrastructure costs, security risk, and organizational redesign — affect small businesses just as much as enterprises, often with less margin for getting it wrong.

What is the agentic AI reality check Deloitte warns about?

Deloitte found that only 11% of organizations have deployed AI agents in production. The barrier is not the technology — it is that most companies automate processes built for people instead of redesigning those processes for AI. The fix requires starting with clean, bounded use cases and building agent-ready workflows from the ground up.

How should small businesses respond to rising AI infrastructure costs?

Be deliberate about what runs where. AI usage is growing faster than costs are falling, and many businesses are building on infrastructure that was never designed for AI workloads. For most small businesses, the practical answer is staying in cloud-based, managed environments until volume demands a hybrid approach — and tracking costs monthly from day one.
