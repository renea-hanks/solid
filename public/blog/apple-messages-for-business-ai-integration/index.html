Apple Messages for Business Bots: How a Business Actually Gets Started

The route Apple documents is Apple Messages for Business, not an iMessage API. A business registers with Apple for free, chooses a Messaging Service Provider (MSP) that connects it to Apple, and runs any bot through that provider with a human agent behind it. I found no Apple documentation that offers a business a direct iMessage API.

People search for this as Apple Business Messages. Apple's current name is Messages for Business, and its older documents call it Business Chat. I checked every Apple rule below against Apple's own pages on October 27, 2026. Where a document is older, I say so.

1. Is Apple Messages for Business the same as iMessage?

No. Apple's FAQ says Messages for Business differs from iMessage by being a business-to-customer service. Apple says the two share the same hosting app, and the encryption differs slightly. It works on devices running iOS 11.3 or later and macOS 10.13.4 or later.

2. Is there an iMessage API a business can use?

I did not find one in Apple's documentation. Apple publishes a REST API for Messaging Service Providers, and its integration page describes it as the provider's tool. Apple's FAQ says a virtual assistant reaches the service through a provider, not directly through that API.

Some vendors sell products they call an iMessage API. I found nothing in Apple's Messages for Business documentation that covers them. Before you build on one, ask the vendor in writing how messages are delivered and whether Apple approves it.

3. How does a business get started?

Apple's FAQ lists the Messaging Service Provider as the first step. You then register your business with Apple at no charge, and Apple reviews it before customers can reach you. Apple's older Getting Started guide (December 2019 edition) lists what registration asks for: organization details, technical and executive contacts, brand information, support hours, and a square logo at least 1024 by 1024 pixels.

Before launch, Apple's Experience Review asks you to test both the automation and the live agent interactions. Your provider uploads a screen recording that shows the live agent and the out-of-hours messages. Apple sends its feedback to the provider.

4. Can an AI bot run on Messages for Business?

Apple allows bots, which its documents call virtual agents. The FAQ recommends a blend of bots and human agents. The pages I reviewed do not address generative AI by name, so ask your provider what it supports and what it has already gotten approved.

A bot on this channel is a piece of a service, not the whole service. Apple's provider guide describes bot flows that triage a conversation and hand it to a live agent with the conversation history.

5. Does Apple require a human agent?

Yes. The FAQ says the option to escalate to a human agent is mandatory, and that Apple will not approve a deployment that uses virtual agents exclusively. Apple's policies document (February 2019 edition) says a business must provide access to a live agent during its regular business hours and must not provide a limited or bot-only solution.

The integration page says the provider's demo must show an automated agent handing a conversation to a live agent.

6. Can a business message customers first?

No. The customer must start the conversation, and Apple says messaging a phone number does not start one. Customers can start from a Messages for Business button on a website, an email, a QR code, or an iOS app.

Apple's policies prohibit unsolicited messages. Marketing or promotional messages require the customer to opt in, and Apple's FAQ says to ask permission before enrolling a customer in push notifications. When a customer ends a conversation, they should not receive more messages unless they start a new one.

7. What can it do beyond plain text?

Apple's documents list interactive objects such as List Pickers, along with Apple Pay and OAuth2 endpoints. Each one has a requirement. Apple Pay requires a merchant account and Apple Developer Program access. OAuth2 endpoints require Apple's approval, which Apple says can take two to three business days.

8. What does it cost?

Registering with Apple is free. Apple's FAQ says the cost of a deployment depends on its scope and on the provider you choose. I am not quoting provider prices, because they vary by provider.

9. What should you decide before you build?

This part is my recommendation, not an Apple rule. In the documents I reviewed, Apple does not require a bot to announce that it is a bot. I recommend it anyway, because a customer who is unsure whether a person is answering stops trusting the channel.

Decide four things in writing before launch. First, the exact words your bot uses to say what it is. Second, who answers when it escalates, and during which hours. Third, what the bot will never claim or promise. Fourth, how you will test it by asking it directly whether it is human. These decisions belong to your AI agent design, not to the technology.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an iMessage API for businesses?

Not one I could find from Apple. Apple documents Messages for Business, a business-to-customer service. A business registers with Apple and connects through a Messaging Service Provider, which uses Apple's REST API on the business's behalf. Services that call themselves an iMessage API are separate from that Apple channel, so ask any vendor in writing how it delivers messages and whether Apple approves.

Can a business run an AI bot on Apple Messages for Business?

Apple allows bots, which its documents call virtual agents, and recommends a blend of bots and human agents. The bot must connect through an approved Messaging Service Provider, not directly to Apple's REST API. The pages I reviewed do not address generative AI specifically, so confirm with your provider what it supports.

Does Apple require a human agent?

Yes. Apple's FAQ says the option to escalate to a human agent is mandatory and that Apple will not approve a deployment that uses virtual agents exclusively. Apple's policies also require access to a live agent during the business's regular hours.

Can a business text customers first on Messages for Business?

No. Apple's FAQ says the customer must start the conversation, and messaging a phone number does not start one. Apple's policies prohibit unsolicited messages, and marketing messages require the customer to opt in.

Does registering with Apple cost anything?

Apple's FAQ says creating a Messages for Business account with Apple is free. The cost of a deployment depends on its scope and on the Messaging Service Provider you choose.
