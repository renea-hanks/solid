Phone Screen Tool

A structured 10-minute screen. Every candidate hears the same questions in the same order, and every candidate is rated only against the job requirements you list. Your work saves as you type.

Read this first

This tool is a note-taking and organizing aid. It is not legal advice, and it does not tell you what is lawful. Rules differ by state, city, and employer size. Check the rules that apply to you. Solid Solutions Today is not a law firm.

A person makes every decision here. This tool does not use AI to score, rank, or reject anyone. If you add AI tools to your hiring process, check the rules where you hire before you do.

The notes you type into this tool are stored only in this browser, on this device, and are not sent to Solid Solutions Today. To move your work to another device, choose Export backup on this one and Import backup on the other.

Step 1

Set up the role

Complete this once per role. The script and the rating section fill in from it.

Job requirements

Enter three to six requirements taken directly from your job posting. Use only the skills, experience, credentials, or duties the job truly needs. These are the only things you will rate.

Step 2

Run the call

Read each question as written, in order, to every candidate. Do not add, skip, or reword questions. Take notes only on what the candidate says about the listed requirements.

Rules for every call

Show or hide the rules

Keep these out of the call: age, date of birth, or graduation year; race or color; religion or religious practices; sex, gender identity, or sexual orientation; pregnancy, plans for children, childcare, or marital or family status; national origin, birthplace, accent, or native language; citizenship or immigration status; disability, health, medications, or medical history; genetic information; arrests; military discharge details; union membership; or current or past pay, benefits, or pay history. This is a general reminder, not a complete or legal list. Your city or state may cover more. Check the rules that apply to you.

Never rate: accent, voice, speech pattern, tone, enthusiasm, personality, appearance, or “fit.” Rate only what the candidate said about the requirements you listed.

If a candidate volunteers any of the above, do not follow up on it and do not write it down. Say: “Thank you. I can only consider the requirements of the role. Let’s continue.”

Never end the call early, and never decide during the call. Complete every question. Decide afterward, in Step 3.

Candidate

Opening

“Hi [candidate name], this is [your name] from [company]. Thank you for applying for the [role] position. This is a short, structured call of about ten minutes. I ask every candidate the same questions in the same order so that everyone is treated the same way. I will be taking notes on your answers. If you need any accommodation to take part in this call, or you would like a different time or format, tell me now and we will arrange it. Is now a good time?”

If the candidate asks for an accommodation or another time: say “Absolutely. I will follow up to arrange that.” Do not ask why. End the call politely and record the candidate in Step 3 as Hold for follow-up.

Question 1  ·  Schedule

“This position is [work arrangement]. The schedule is [schedule and start date]. Are you able to work that schedule?”

If yes: continue. If no, or if the candidate mentions any need or limitation: say “Thank you for telling me. I will note that and follow up after the call.” Do not ask why. Do not end the call. Continue to the next question.

Question 2  ·  Essential duties

“The essential duties of this role include the following: [essential duties]. Are you able to perform these duties, with or without reasonable accommodation?”

If yes: continue. If the candidate has a question or raises a concern: say “Thank you. I will note that and follow up after the call.” Do not ask about health, disability, or medical conditions. Do not ask why.

Question 3  ·  Pay range

“The pay range for this role is [pay range]. Does that range work for you?”

If yes or no: say “Thank you. I will note that.” and continue. Never ask what the candidate earns now or earned before. If the candidate volunteers it, say “I can’t use that information in this process. Let’s focus on the range for this role,” and do not write it down.

Questions 4 and on  ·  Experience with each requirement

Ask one question for each requirement you listed in Step 1. Rate the candidate on what they actually described.

How to rate. Meets: the candidate described direct experience that matches the requirement, with specifics. Partially meets: the candidate described related experience, but with a clear gap against the requirement. Does not meet: after being asked, the candidate described no relevant experience. Not enough information: the question was not answered or there was no time. Follow up before you decide.

Candidate questions

“What questions do you have about the role?”

Answer only with facts about the job that you have prepared. Do not make promises about hiring, pay beyond the stated range, or timing beyond what you have decided.

Close

“Thank you, [candidate name]. If this moves forward, a full interview would take place [timeframe]. I will be in touch by [date] either way. Thank you for your time.”

Step 3

Decide after the call

Record the results first, then choose an outcome. A schedule answer, a pay answer, or a question about the duties never ends a candidacy by itself. Those go to follow-up and review.

Question results

Question 1: schedule
Question 2: essential duties
Question 3: pay range

Outcome

Reasons Choose only requirements rated Partially meets or Does not meet. Required if you choose Do not advance.

Step 4

Saved screens

Keep a record of every candidate you screen for this role, including those you advance.

Record-keeping rules differ by place and employer size. Check the period that applies to you before you delete anything. Treat saved notes about candidates as confidential.
