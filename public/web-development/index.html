Web Development & The Truth About Search

Search changed. Almost nobody told small business owners how much.

The Reality

Most Google searches don't send anyone to your website anymore.

In the first four months of 2026, 68.01% of U.S. Google searches ended without a single click to any website, up from 60.45% in 2024 (SparkToro, using Similarweb clickstream data, reported June 2026).

That means the search itself gets answered, and the person never leaves Google. Your business can be accurate, complete, and exactly what they were looking for, and still get zero visits, because the answer was already delivered on the results page.

What "zero-click" actually looks like

SparkToro's research found AI Overviews on more than 20% of Google searches, with click-through rates dropping by nearly 60% when one appears. Semrush found that 92% to 94% of AI Mode sessions end without a click to an outside website (both as reported by TechWyse, June 2026). The traffic isn't going to a competitor. It's often not going anywhere.

Domain Authority

Brand mentions now track AI visibility more closely than backlinks do.

For years, the advice was simple: build backlinks, raise your domain authority, and rankings follow. Ahrefs analyzed 75,000 brands and found that brand web mentions correlate with AI Overview brand visibility at 0.664, the strongest of the factors it tested. Domain Rating correlated at 0.326, referring domains at 0.295, and backlinks at 0.218.

Ahrefs notes that correlation is not causation, and so do we. The study does show where the signal is strongest: whether other places on the web talk about your business, not only whether other sites link to it.

The Map Pack

Distance is one of three local ranking factors, and you can't change it.

Google says local results are ranked on relevance, distance, and prominence. Distance is one of the three, so a competitor who is closer to the searcher starts ahead on that factor no matter how good your website is. That is a structural limit, not a content problem, and no amount of blog writing fixes it.

Relevance and prominence are where a business can act. Google says a verified, complete Business Profile with accurate information, and more reviews with positive ratings, can help local ranking. That work belongs to the business, and it is part of what gets built here.

Where Search Actually Happens Now

Your customer might ask Google's AI Mode, ChatGPT, or Claude instead, and each one finds businesses differently.

Google announced at I/O on May 20, 2026 that AI Mode has passed 1 billion monthly users and that Gemini 3.5 Flash is its new default model worldwide.

Outside Google, a real share of buyers ask AI chatbots directly. G2's March 2026 survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers found that 51% now start research in an AI chatbot more often than in Google, up from 29% the year before, and 71% rely on one at some point. That survey covers software buyers, not every kind of customer.

Google AI Mode / Gemini

Google says no special optimization is needed beyond the fundamentals of Search: pages that are indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, content in plain text, and structured data that matches the page.

ChatGPT

OpenAI uses OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT search and a separate crawler, GPTBot, for training. Blocking GPTBot does not remove a site from ChatGPT search. Allowing OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt lets search reach your site.

Claude

Anthropic uses Claude-SearchBot for search, ClaudeBot for training, and Claude-User for requests a person makes. Allowing Claude-SearchBot in robots.txt lets Claude's search reach your site.

The Part No One Wants to Say

You can do everything right and still not be the recommended answer.

A fast site. Full schema markup. AEO-structured content. Blog posts and FAQs that answer real questions in plain language. All of it done correctly, the way it's supposed to be done — and it is still possible to not be the business an AI system names, because the deciding signals now include things a website alone doesn't control: brand mentions across other people's sites, consistency across directories you don't own, review volume and recency, and which specific AI system someone happened to ask.

That's not a reason to stop doing the technical work — the data above shows it still matters, just less on its own than it used to. It's a reason to stop believing that a well-built website is a guarantee of anything. It's a foundation, not a finish line.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a fast, well-built website enough to be found in AI search now?

No. Speed, schema, and good content still matter, but they are not the whole picture. Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands found that brand web mentions correlate with AI Overview brand visibility at 0.664, ahead of Domain Rating at 0.326 and backlinks at 0.218. A technically excellent site can still be passed over if the business is rarely mentioned elsewhere on the web. Correlation is not proof of cause.

Why does my service-area business struggle to show up in the map pack?

Google says local results are ranked on relevance, distance, and prominence. Distance is one of the three, so a closer competitor starts ahead on that factor regardless of your website. A verified, complete Business Profile and genuine reviews help the other two.

How does ChatGPT find my business?

OpenAI uses OAI-SearchBot to find sites for ChatGPT search and a separate crawler, GPTBot, for training. Blocking GPTBot does not remove a site from ChatGPT search. To be considered in ChatGPT search, allow OAI-SearchBot in your robots.txt.

How does Claude find my business?

Anthropic uses separate crawlers: Claude-SearchBot for search, ClaudeBot for training, and Claude-User for requests a person makes. Allowing Claude-SearchBot in your robots.txt lets Claude's search reach your site.

Is AI replacing Google search?

Not by itself, but the shift is real. Google says AI Mode has passed 1 billion monthly users. G2's March 2026 survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers found 51% now start research in an AI chatbot more often than in Google, up from 29% the year before. That survey covers software buyers, not every kind of customer.
