Web Development & The Truth About Search

Search changed. Almost nobody told small business owners how much.

The Reality

Most Google searches don't send anyone to your website anymore.

In the first four months of 2026, 68.01% of U.S. Google searches ended without a single click to any website — up from 60.45% just two years earlier, the fastest two-year jump on record (SparkToro/Similarweb, June 2026). When an AI Overview appears in the results, that number climbs to roughly 83%. In Google's AI Mode, it's closer to 93%.

That means the search itself gets answered — and the person never leaves Google. Your business can be accurate, complete, and exactly what they were looking for, and still get zero visits, because the answer was already delivered on the results page.

What "zero-click" actually looks like

AI Overviews now appear on roughly 43–48% of U.S. Google searches (Similarweb/BrightEdge, 2026), more than triple the coverage from a year earlier. When one appears, organic click-through rate drops by around 61% compared to a search with no AI Overview at all (Similarweb Zero-Click Study). The traffic isn't going to a competitor. It's often not going anywhere.

Domain Authority

The metric you were told to chase for a decade stopped predicting what it used to.

For years, the advice was simple: build backlinks, raise your domain authority, and rankings follow. That relationship is breaking down for AI-driven search. A 2026 analysis of citation signals across major AI platforms found domain authority correlates with AI citation probability at only about r=0.18 — while brand mentions across the web, independent of any link, correlate at r=0.664 (Wellows/LumenGEO, 2026).

Separately, research across more than 22,000 domains found only 7.2% overlap between the sites Google's AI Overviews cite and the sites large language models like ChatGPT and Claude cite for the same topics. They are, in large part, reading from two different libraries.

Ranking position has weakened too. In mid-2025, about 76% of AI Overview citations came from a page in Google's top 10 organic results. By early 2026, that had dropped to somewhere between 17% and 38%, depending on the study (Ahrefs, BrightEdge, SeoClarity). Ranking #1 today gives you roughly a one-in-three chance of being the one cited.

The Map Pack

If your business isn't a storefront, Google's local system is working against you from the start.

Google's local ranking system still weighs proximity to a single registered physical address heavily — even when a business legitimately serves an entire region. A service-area business without a storefront is compared against businesses with a fixed location, using a system that was built around the fixed location. That's a structural disadvantage, not a content problem, and no amount of blog writing fixes it.

This hits rural businesses two ways at once. There's less local search volume to be found in overall, and when a search does happen, the map pack itself favors whoever sits closest to the searcher's device — which, in a sparsely populated area, might be a competitor two towns over who simply has a better-optimized Google Business Profile, or no competitor at all, in which case the business is invisible not because it did anything wrong, but because Google's local layer wasn't built with rural service areas in mind.

BrightLocal's 2026 research found only 44% of businesses appear in the map pack at all for their own primary keyword — meaning more than half of local businesses, including many with solid websites, are simply absent from the most visible real estate in local search.

Where Search Actually Happens Now

Google still owns most of it. But your customer might be asking Gemini, ChatGPT, or Claude instead — and each one works differently.

Google still holds roughly 90% of global search market share as of mid-2026 (StatCounter). That has not collapsed. What has changed is what happens inside that share: Gemini 3.5 Flash became the default model powering both AI Overviews and Google's AI Mode worldwide as of Google's May 2026 I/O announcement, and AI Mode alone passed 1 billion monthly active users within its first year.

Outside of Google, a real and growing share of buyers are asking AI chatbots directly instead of searching at all. 51% of B2B software buyers now start their research in an AI chatbot more often than Google, and 71% use one somewhere in the process (G2 Answer Economy report, April 2026). McKinsey's 2026 research found 44% of AI search users now call it their preferred source for buying decisions — ahead of traditional search engines, brand websites, and review sites combined.

Google AI Mode / Gemini

Runs on Google's own index. Rewards structured data, schema, and E-E-A-T signals — but citation is decoupled from classic ranking position more than ever.

ChatGPT

For local business questions, it leans heavily on Google itself. One 2026 study of 1,732 citations found it referenced google.com roughly 60 times more often than the next domain — reading your Google Business Profile back to the user, not your site.

Claude

Behaves more like a research assistant. It cross-references multiple independent sources — your site, your GBP, Yelp, directories — and looks for consensus before naming a business with confidence. Consistency across sources matters more than any single one.

The Part No One Wants to Say

You can do everything right and still not be the recommended answer.

A fast site. Full schema markup. AEO-structured content. Blog posts and FAQs that answer real questions in plain language. All of it done correctly, the way it's supposed to be done — and it is still possible to not be the business an AI system names, because the deciding signals now include things a website alone doesn't control: brand mentions across other people's sites, consistency across directories you don't own, review volume and recency, and which specific AI system someone happened to ask.

That's not a reason to stop doing the technical work — the data above shows it still matters, just less on its own than it used to. It's a reason to stop believing that a well-built website is a guarantee of anything. It's a foundation, not a finish line.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a fast, well-built website enough to be found in AI search now?

No. Speed, schema, AEO structure, and good content still matter, but 2026 research shows domain authority now correlates with AI citation at only about r=0.18, while off-site brand mentions correlate at r=0.664. A technically excellent site can still be passed over if it lacks corroborating mentions elsewhere on the web.

Why does my service-area business struggle to show up in the map pack?

Google's local ranking system still anchors proximity to a registered physical address. Service-area businesses without a storefront face a structural disadvantage in that calculation, regardless of review count or website quality. BrightLocal's 2026 data found only 44% of businesses appear in the map pack for their own primary keyword at all.

How does ChatGPT decide which local business to recommend?

ChatGPT's local answers are grounded heavily in Google's own data, not your website. A 2026 scrape of 1,732 AI citations found ChatGPT cited google.com roughly 60 times more often than any other single domain, rendering Google Business Profile fields — name, hours, rating, category — directly into its answers.

Does Claude work the same way as ChatGPT for local recommendations?

Not exactly. Claude behaves more like a research assistant, cross-referencing multiple independent sources — your website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, directories — and looking for consensus before naming a business with confidence. Businesses with consistent, structured information across more sources get cited more often.

How much of search is still controlled by Google?

Google still holds roughly 90% of global search market share as of mid-2026. Gemini 3.5 Flash is now the default model powering both AI Overviews and Google's AI Mode worldwide, and AI Mode alone passed 1 billion monthly users by May 2026. Google's decisions about how it ranks and cites content still shape the majority of how small businesses are found.
